# Threads and public sources on the pain (15.11, ~00:40)

**Goal:** find what people outside the App Store say about a pay-later payment that fails before the paycheck and a limit cut after it.
**What worked and what didn't:** Reddit is closed to our search tool and to the built-in browser (blocked), so there are **no Reddit quotes** here; Beksultan's Google autocomplete (`search-demand.md`) shows people search "…reddit" for exactly this. Below are sources we could read, from strongest to weakest.

## 1. Afterpay's own help page (US) — confirmed, read in full
[I missed a payment. What happens to my account?](https://www.afterpay.com/en-US/help/900005359743-I-missed-a-payment-What-happens-to-my-account)
- *"we will immediately pause your account"* — no new purchases until payments are up to date.
- *"You may also incur late fees."*
- *"your spending limit has decreased too"* — repayment history is one of the main factors.
**Use on the slide:** the lender itself says one missed payment pauses the account and can cut the limit. This is the mechanism Guard prevents.

## 2. App Store reviews — confirmed (see `reviews.md`)
- *"Missed a $7 payment by 5 hours… my spending power went from 1000 to 200."* (Afterpay)
- *"they try to take a payment off your card at 6 in the morning"* — paycheck at 3–4 pm the same day (Afterpay, review 14003423669).
- ~1 in 10 one- and two-star reviews (617) are about a failed payment, a cut limit or a refund (keyword count).

## 3. Lawsuits against Klarna — NOT verified (the article answered 403)
Search summaries of [Top Class Actions, 2022](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/money/klarna-class-action-alleges-buy-now-pay-later-claim-misleads-customers) describe US class actions alleging that Klarna debits caused bank overdraft fees ($29 after a $42.84 debit, $35 after a $15.83 debit) and that Klarna targets people living paycheck to paycheck. **Allegations, and we could not open the page.** Don't put the numbers on a slide unless someone opens the article and checks them.

## 4. Lender rules that shape our advice — secondary sources
- Affirm charges no late fees, but a late payment can lower purchasing power, and the due date can't be extended after purchase (NerdWallet's Affirm review; Gerald blog — Gerald sells a competing product, so read with care). This is why Guard advises "move money tonight" or "pay by hand after payday" and never promises a date change.
- A consumer blog notes the paycheck can land hours before or after the lender's debit attempt (Gerald, commercial bias). It agrees with the App Store quote above.
- An Australian forum (ProductReview.com.au) has a user whose limit dropped to zero after paying one day late. It's not US, so use it only as color.

## What this changes
- **For the deck:** pair the 5-hours review quote with Afterpay's own words (*"your spending limit has decreased too"*). One is the user, the other is the lender confirming the rule.
- **Still missing:** a real US voice from a thread or the survey (#16). Reddit has to be read by a person in a normal browser.
